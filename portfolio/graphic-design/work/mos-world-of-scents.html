A crowded perfume flyer, rebuilt to get shop owners calling

Fourteen products, four typefaces and a pink gradient were competing for attention. The redesign gives the flyer one job: get retailers to call and buy in bulk.

Client
Mo's World of Scents, Lagos
My role
Art direction, layout, typography
Time
6 days
Delivered
Print flyer, 4:5 post, story version

The brief

Mo sells original perfumes wholesale to boutiques, shops and online resellers. The old flyer went out on WhatsApp and Instagram and was handed out at markets. People liked it, but they didn't call. Mo wanted a flyer that made the business look like a serious supplier.

The problem

Everything on the original was the same volume, so the eye had nowhere to land. The offer (buy in bulk, resell, earn) was buried under product shots, and on a phone the number was the smallest thing on the page.

What was holding it back

  1. Four typefaces. An ornate serif, a script, a condensed bold and a sans, all at similar sizes, so nothing reads as the headline.
  2. Three badges competing. The bulk-buy seal, the sales box and the logo all used the same maroon and gold.
  3. Fourteen products. A whole catalogue squeezed in. Shop owners couldn't tell what Mo specialises in.
  4. A buried phone number. The one detail that matters most was crammed into the footer.

Four decisions that changed it

Lead with the business, not the bottles

Retailers buy a margin. “Wholesale Fragrance” became three times the size of anything else, and the benefits moved into one dark panel you can scan in five seconds.

Cut the range to five

Five amber bottles and jars say “supplier” better than fourteen mixed brands. It also removed the risk of showing other companies' perfumes.

Warm and quiet instead of pink and gold

Pink and gold read as a gift shop. Cream, espresso and amber read as a supplier with stock. The orange badge is the only loud thing, so the offer is seen first.

Make calling the obvious next step

The number got its own outlined button at the bottom, large enough to read in a WhatsApp preview without opening the image.

What happened next

In the first two weeks Mo got three times as many WhatsApp inquiries as the month before. The layout has since been reused for 12 follow-up posts, and the print-ready files were delivered six days after the brief.
