Mo's World of Scents: turning a crowded flyer into a wholesale sales tool

Fourteen products, four typefaces and a pink gradient were competing for attention. The redesign gives the flyer one job: get shop owners to call and buy in bulk.

Client
Mo's World of Scents, Lagos
My role
Art direction, layout, typography
Timeline
6 days
Deliverables
Print flyer, 4:5 social post, story version

The brief

Mo sells original perfumes wholesale to boutiques, shops and online resellers. The existing flyer was shared on WhatsApp and Instagram, and handed out at markets. People liked it but didn't call. Mo wanted a flyer that looked like a serious supplier, so retailers would trust the deal.

The problem

Everything on the original was the same volume. The eye had nowhere to land, and the message (buy in bulk, resell, earn) was buried under product shots. On a phone screen the phone number was the smallest thing on the page.

What was holding it back

  1. Four typefaces. Ornate serif, script, condensed bold and a sans, all at similar sizes. No clear headline.
  2. Three badges fighting. The bulk-buy seal, the sales box and the logo all used the same maroon and gold, so none of them stood out.
  3. Fourteen products. A catalogue squeezed into a flyer. Shop owners couldn't tell what Mo actually specialises in.
  4. A buried phone number. The single most important detail was crammed into the footer.

Key decisions

Lead with the business, not the bottles

Retailers buy a margin. The headline became “Wholesale Fragrance” at three times the size of anything else, with the benefits stacked in one dark panel they can scan in five seconds.

Cut the range to five

Five amber bottles and jars say “supplier” better than fourteen mixed brands. It also removed the copyright risk of showing other companies' perfumes.

Warm, quiet, premium

Pink and gold read as gift shop. Cream, espresso and amber read as a supplier with stock. The orange badge is the only loud element, so the offer is the first thing you see.

Make calling the obvious next step

The phone number moved into its own outlined button at the bottom, set large enough to read on a WhatsApp preview without opening the image.
